# 02 — Strategy, Customer Jobs and Complete Experience Journeys

## Product promise
**Put emerging innovation where opportunity can find it.** Every university student, builder or researcher with something worth showing should be able to create a remarkable, discoverable project profile even if they have no registered company, pitch deck, followers or investor network. Discovery should lead to verified contacts, useful feedback, collaboration, opportunities and growth rather than end with likes.

## Product category and boundary
- **Primary:** university-first innovation discovery and showcase network.
- **Secondary:** innovation portfolio, progress journal, identity/provenance and matchmaking tools.
- **Professional:** investor/industry/university scouting, pipeline and curated introductions.
- **Later conditional:** financing readiness, licensing, regulated investment partner referrals.
- **Not:** a securities exchange, patent registrar, automatic money-valuing machine, unrestricted source-code marketplace or generic AI chat site.

## Main personas and jobs-to-be-done

### P1 — Student with project but no startup company
**Situation:** I made a capstone prototype, research project, app, machine, agricultural process or design. I want people beyond my lecturer to see it.
**Job:** In under 15 minutes, convert photographs, a report and a brief explanation into a trustworthy, attractive public card and project page; choose safe visibility; share it by link or QR code.
**Value:** recognition, collaborators, internships/commissioned pilots, university interest, funding/competitions when suitable.
**Big risk:** accidental disclosure of patentable or university-owned information.
**Moment of delight:** a verified engineer or university innovation office requests a demo.

### P2 — Student team becoming a startup
**Job:** Present team and milestones, make progress visible, recruit skills, capture qualified interest and move from prototype to credible venture.
**Value:** founders can be discovered on evidence instead of social reach.
**Big risk:** team ownership conflicts, unverified traction or false investment expectations.

### P3 — Industry scout/manufacturer
**Job:** Find workable solutions to a named industrial problem with a clear technology stage, test evidence, location, collaboration terms and usable contact pathway.
**Value:** less searching and fewer dead-end pitches.
**Moment of delight:** shortlist three relevant, technically credible projects and invite two to pilot discussions.

### P4 — University innovation director
**Job:** With permission, assemble an accurate public showcase, verify university association, manage cohorts and challenges, log outcomes and support commercialization.
**Value:** stronger reach beyond campus; reportable evidence of partnerships and spinouts.
**Big risk:** accidental inference of university endorsement or exposure of confidential student research.

### P5 — Investor or accelerator manager
**Job:** Discover stage/sector-matched founders and obtain structured, permissioned evidence for qualification.
**Value:** higher-quality pipeline, lower diligence friction, progress monitoring.
**Big risk:** manipulated metrics or pitches masquerading as verified business performance.

### P6 — Mentor, cofounder, alumni or skilled collaborator
**Job:** Find an exciting project where a specific skill is needed and connect without spamming.
**Value:** meaningful participation, reputation based on real delivered work.

### P7 — General public/press
**Job:** See inspiring projects, understand them quickly, share safe summaries and follow breakthroughs.
**Value:** stories worth sharing; public discovery should be allowed without mandatory ID collection.

## A central strategic correction: exposure is the hero, not investment
Place **Discover** first in the public navigation. Place **Launch Project** as primary creator CTA. Keep investors as one audience among mentors, universities, customers, manufacturers and press. Fundraising is one opportunity type, not the umbrella experience. Full national-ID verification at first browse will wreck discovery; require progressive checks only for privileged actions and restricted access.

## Five foundational use journeys

### Journey A — public visitor to project follower
1. Open a search-visible or QR-linked university project card.
2. Immediately see project hero, plain-English value, stage, university association status and demo.
3. Browse public-safe gallery, progress and verified claims without joining.
4. Save/follow or request introduction: lightweight account creation prompted here.
5. Receive opt-in milestone updates; discover adjacent projects, not endless unrelated ads.
**Acceptance condition:** public visitors can meaningfully evaluate a non-confidential project without surrendering identity documents.

### Journey B — student document to public launch
1. Select `I built something` → project type: research / prototype / software / hardware / early startup / creative technology / other.
2. Add title, one-line purpose, sector, location, stage, collaboration needs.
3. Add optional files (photo/video/report/pitch/GitHub). Save a private draft immediately.
4. AI suggests a short public explanation, tags, gallery order and optional summary; user edits and approves.
5. Critical **safe-to-publish checkpoint** flags sensitive code, techniques, patent-worthy details, personal contact information and uncertain institutional rights.
6. Choose: `Private`, `Public teaser only`, `Public detailed profile`, `Approved viewers only` for specific assets.
7. Preview exactly what non-registered visitors see; publish; generate social/WhatsApp sharing image and QR code.
8. Get a personal dashboard showing relevant discovery, follows, requests and actual outcomes.
**Acceptance:** No sensitive upload moves from draft to public by default, and rollback/visibility revocation is available (not guaranteed to erase earlier copies).

### Journey C — industry contact / useful introduction
1. Search e.g. `solar-powered cold storage field-tested Zimbabwe`.
2. Filter: proof stage, geography, technology, collaboration goal, university affiliation.
3. Compare public projects by specific evidence and **claim verification level**, not arbitrary startup price.
4. Open a project: `Request demo / Explore pilot / Offer manufacturing / Ask question`.
5. Complete short professional profile and a structured, abuse-resistant request with intended purpose.
6. Founder or authorized project steward accepts/declines; both see privacy and rights reminders.
7. Meeting and follow-up outcome can be recorded with both parties' consent.
**Acceptance:** User cannot open confidential source files merely by calling themselves an investor.

### Journey D — university demo day
1. Authorized campus organizer creates `University Expo 2027` and invites projects with permission.
2. Teams receive a guided project checklist; each has a public-safe showcase page.
3. Organizer curates sectors, live demo schedule, QR cards and featured galleries.
4. Visitors search projects and register for event attendance; exhibitors can choose interest categories.
5. Judges submit rubric scores privately; public badges only if approved.
6. Post-event reports show verified participants and outcomes with aggregation/small-cell privacy safeguards.
**Acceptance:** Event participation does not transfer ownership or automatically disclose restricted technical material.

### Journey E — graduate into professional financing
1. A maturing business uploads requested corporate/financial materials privately.
2. Readiness checklist tracks incorporation, team governance, financial statements, product traction and risk disclosures.
3. Authorized professional requests access; verified permissions and appropriate agreements apply.
4. Platform offers introduction to established financing channels/regulated partners where legal/contractually permitted.
5. Actual securities solicitation, KYC/AML, custody, subscription and settlement remain with duly authorized entities.
**Acceptance:** No buying/selling of shares occurs in the unlicensed discovery platform.

## Behavior design: why every group comes back
- **Innovators:** weekly progress journal, engagement *quality* not vanity likes, new matched opportunities, collaborator replies, practical checklist.
- **Professional scouts:** saved smart searches, credible evidence updates, a shortlist/workspace, responsive contact system.
- **Universities:** portfolio overview, reusable showcase pages, reporting, opt-in verified outcomes.
- **Public:** short demo explainers and stories, personalized topic follows, authentic achievements.
- **Mentors:** curated requests matching explicit skills, recognition tied to completed collaborations.

## Relevance over virality
Not all users need identical feeds. A mechanical engineer should be able to view engineering prototypes; a grant programme should find eligible teams; an investor should see ventures within mandate. Popularity may signal interest but never replace credibility.

## Trust levels (orthogonal, not a single fake badge)
1. **Identity check:** unverified / email / student affiliation / ID-verified professional.
2. **Project association:** self-declared / university-linked / institution-attested.
3. **Claim evidence:** unsubstantiated / linked document / third-party supported / independently reviewed.
4. **IP status:** founder-declared / team-declared / joint/uncertain / rights documents reviewed; the system does not adjudicate ownership.
5. **Readiness:** idea / research / prototype / tested / pilots / live product / revenue / scaling, each with evidence and update timestamps.

## Go-to-market promise should be measurable
Avoid claims like 'we are the stock market of ideas.' Say instead: **'Publish your project. Show real progress. Be discovered by the people who can take it further.'** Verify actual ability to connect both sides before marketing investment opportunities.

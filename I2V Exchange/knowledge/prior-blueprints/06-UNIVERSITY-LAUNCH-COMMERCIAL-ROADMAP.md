# 06 — University-First Launch, Partnership, Business and Validation Plan

## The right sequence: create an audience before selling sophistication
An 80-feature app with no real projects or professional attention will fail the central user promise. The first problem is marketplace liquidity: **enough demonstrable projects in a focused domain**, plus **enough qualified discoverers** to generate actual useful contacts. Build that before elaborate AI valuations, stock-like graphs or institutional procurement.

## Beachhead
Start with a *small curated pilot* across **2–3 university communities or innovation clubs** and perhaps a single thematic sector such as agritech/energy/engineering, subject to partnership access. Do not launch simultaneously across every Zimbabwean university and 30 industries. The pilot can be digital-first without implying formal institution endorsement.

## Suggested pilot prospect map (not partnerships)
- **University of Zimbabwe Innovation Hub:** graduate innovator and fellows programmes; student and IP workflows.
- **NUST Innovation & Business Development:** technology-transfer, prototyping and industry bridge.
- **Midlands State University Innovation Unit:** research, incubation and community projects.
- **Harare Institute of Technology:** active technology and student venture development.
- **Bindura University of Science Education:** innovation programming.
- **BOOST/Enactus Zimbabwe:** potential student recruitment and competitions.
- **NIAC/POTRAZ:** potential future ecosystem integration, grant/program discovery.
- **ZEEX / FINSEC:** potential long-term qualification and finance-stage handoffs under appropriate approvals; not a pilot requirement.

## Stakeholder-specific partnership pitches
**Campus director:** “Create a beautifully presented opt-in showcase of projects with safe publishing controls and qualified external contacts; track relevant outcomes.”  
**Student:** “No investor connections needed. Turn what you built into a professional project page, get discovered and find support.”  
**Industry scout:** “Search campus innovations by real problem and demonstrated stage; request demos from the people who built them.”  
**Investor:** “Find ventures earlier than usual, with clearer documentary and project context.”  
**Alumni mentor:** “Contribute directly to tangible projects that need your expertise.”

## Pilot recruitment and fairness
- Recruit innovation-hub coordinators, student club leaders, lab supervisors and engineering/project capstone teams.
- Onboard a *curated cohort of 40–80 real projects* as a test target, not a market-size prediction.
- Seek *10–20 professional reviewers or potential collaborators* aligned by sector; actual willingness unproven.
- Help founders build good demos from their actual photos, prototypes and reports; do not ghostwrite fictitious achievements.
- Avoid institutions acquiring automatic rights to a student's project page as a condition of participation.
- Include non-university independent innovators in a separate admissions path so the product remains broad.

## Validation schedule (first 90 days)
**Days 1–21 — Interviews & observation:** 20 student teams, 5 campus innovation staff, 10 external discoverers. Observe them using current tools (LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Google Drive, university sites). Ask them to complete concrete tasks, not endorse a futuristic pitch. Survey who owns project assets and publication policies. Deliver a ranked list of user problems.

**Days 22–45 — Concierge showcase:** Before full coding, recruit ~15 actual project teams (with consent), design manual high-quality pages/prototypes and send to selected professionals. Instrument profile opens, qualified contact requests, founder response and completed meetings. Test safe-disclosure warning and publication flow.

**Days 46–70 — Usability prototype:** Build responsive home/explore/profile/create/request, use realistic anonymized content, run mobile-device tests and ask participants to publish without help. Check discoverer search quality and privacy comprehension.

**Days 71–90 — Partner pilot decision:** Review demand and outcomes: signups alone don't count; count completed intros, demos, interested institutional pilots and repeat project activity. Decide to continue, narrow scope or revise positioning based on evidence.

## Suggested success metrics — hypotheses, not forecasts
**North-star metric:** `Qualified opportunity connections per active credible project per quarter` (qualified means accepted by the project owner and relevant to a stated need).

**Funnel:** visits → project views → saved/followed → structured request → accepted request → meeting/demo → pilot/collaboration/award.  
**Creator:** draft→safe published profile conversion; median creation time; 30-/90-day project updates.  
**Professional:** relevance of search; ratio of credible projects to junk; repeat search and successful intros.  
**Institution:** opt-in programme projects; verification turnaround; partner satisfaction; renewals.  
**Trust:** unauthorized disclosure incidents, false claims corrected, dispute time-to-resolution, rate of meaningful reports.  
**Reach fairness:** exposure distribution across new projects, universities, regions and sectors.

Provisional targets for usability experiments (adjust with evidence):
- >80% of recruited students can create a **private** project in a guided test without support.
- >65% can create and approve a safe public teaser after onboarding; a low score signals design problems.
- >50% of professionals shown a sector-matched set say that at least one project is worth a specific follow-up.
- Confirm at least 5 genuinely accepted intros or meaningful demos before expanding the feature set.
These numbers are internal **experiment thresholds**, not sourced market statistics or promises.

## Commercial strategy
**Free creator:** public showcase, updates, basic discovery, introduction inbox. Core exposure should not be locked behind payment.  
**Student premium later (optional):** small bounded features such as advanced presentation or team tools only if users demonstrate willingness to pay; never demand fees to publish essential work.  
**Institution:** cohort/showcase administration, approved verification, private reporting, branding, event tools, staff seats; subscription/pilot contracts.  
**Corporate:** sector scouting, verified challenges, leads and project evaluation workflows.  
**Investor/accelerator:** filtered search, notes, watchlists, data-room/admin features and professional pipelines.  
**Event services:** digital demo-day packages, carefully disclosed sponsorship.

Avoid depending on commissions from investments (complex regulation) or on selling student project data (trust destruction). For each paid product, measure the time it saves or opportunity it creates.

## Honest expansion framework
**Phase 0:** discovery interviews, prototype and privacy/legal scoping.  
**Phase 1 (pilot):** public-safe projects, excellent search/card/detail UX, creator onboarding, intro workflow, first campus showcases.  
**Phase 2 (retention):** project updates, follows, verified affiliations, smart opportunity board, university dashboards, professional curation.  
**Phase 3 (trust/institutions):** full passport UI, evidence reviews, data rooms, institution workflows, higher assurance and paid pilots.  
**Phase 4 (intelligence):** evidence-grounded AI intake/pitch, sector-specific assessment, collaboration matching, research links and ecosystem reporting.  
**Phase 5 (scale):** dedicated university and industry contracts, broad cross-border discovery, APIs and data products, licensing/intellectual-asset introductions.
**Phase 6 (separate, optional):** investment-readiness and financial integrations only after legal clearance and partner agreements; direct exchange activity only if separately authorized.

Timelines should be based on resourcing and achieved quality gates rather than calendar marketing. The earlier 24–36 month horizon can support substantial progress, but not every speculative feature is required to declare an excellent product.

## Build vs buy vs partner
**Build:** distinctive discovery interface, project/creator records, permissioned previews, interests/intro workflow, university showcases and evidence-oriented search.  
**Buy/use managed services:** auth, file storage, transactional email, analytics, queue, deployment, video hosting; do not self-build commodities prematurely.  
**Partner:** identity assurance, e-signatures, academic verification, authorized financing, professional IP/legal support, selected innovation programmes.

## Largest risk register
| Risk | Early warning | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Many uploads but no interest | low qualified request rate | narrow sectors, recruit discoverers first, improve descriptions and matching |
| Empty directory | no actual demo media or inconsistent quality | curated cohorts and hands-on onboarding |
| IP publication problem | founders don't understand what went public | private default, previews and WIPO-informed guidance |
| Institutional ownership disputes | staff and students disagree about authority | explicit declaration, affiliation ≠ ownership, conflict process |
| Fake claims/funding signals | false badges, inflated charts | provenance categories, review controls, report and appeal |
| Security leakage | confidential material appears in search/media | server ACL, separated indexes, professional testing |
| Regulatory drift | user perceives profiles as tradable securities | no prices/buy buttons or financial solicitations in MVP |
| High data costs | mobile bounce and unfinished uploads | PWA, compress/defer, offline drafts, text-first option |
| Platform captured by sponsors | rankings reflect payments | explicit sponsored labels, editorial governance |
| AI agent errors | fictional project accomplishments | source-grounded extraction and founder approval |
| University contract dependence | long procurement with no usage | student-club and opt-in founder path alongside institutional sales |

## The market-entry sentence
**“A home for the innovations coming out of our universities — built to help the right people see, understand and support them.”**

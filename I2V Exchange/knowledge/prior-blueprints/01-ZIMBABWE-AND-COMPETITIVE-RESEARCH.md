# 01 — Zimbabwe Market & Competitive Research
**Research cutoff: 27 October 2026.** Distinguish product design implications from verified facts.

## Finding A — Zimbabwe did launch an entrepreneurship exchange
Zimbabwe launched the **Zimbabwe Entrepreneurship Exchange (ZEEX)** in **Bulawayo on 24 July 2026**, after the **Securities and Exchange Commission of Zimbabwe** gave ZSE approval in June 2026. The initiative belongs to the **Zimbabwe Stock Exchange** group. It is for SMEs/emerging growth enterprises seeking regulated financing pathways; it is not an open social-style directory where any student prototype is automatically a tradable security. Multiple contemporaneous reports confirm the date and structure. Sources: NewsDay, 27 July 2026; Herald, 2 August 2026; Zimbabwe Mail, 28 July 2026.

Reported pillars: **ZEEX Private Markets** (structured private capital raises), **ZEEX Public Markets** (public issuance, listing and later trading), **Invoice Discounting** (working capital against approved receivables), and **Bondholding Company** (structured collateral/debt access). July reporting indicated an initial **regulatory sandbox** with reviews after six and twelve months; do not infer that every announced facility is already generally available at scale. September coverage referenced InvoiceX, the invoice-discounting pillar; milestone timelines differ in some news accounts, so use 24 July for the well-corroborated official launch, not a date for individual product go-live.

**Product consequence:** We should not launch a counterfeit ZEEX or promise investment access without authorization. We can be upstream: spotlight **students and pre-company inventions** long before they meet exchange eligibility and potentially help credible teams prepare for later finance. Partnership possibilities include referrals and investment-readiness data exchange only under agreement/consent.

## Finding B — Zimbabwe already has private-market discovery infrastructure
FINSEC's official Private Markets page describes GEM Portal for enterprise/investor matching, screening, pipeline building, value discovery, funding, OTC trading, incubation, acceleration and financing/adviser access. This is a closer competitor to parts of our proposed investor/valuation journey than previously assumed.

**Product consequence:** Do not build a generic investor directory and call it unique. Differentiate around **campus-native origination**, pre-incorporation projects, the *quality of demos and progress updates*, rigorous claims/evidence labeling, custom university showcases and discovery-to-collaboration conversion. Professional investment readiness should connect to regulated operators rather than pretending to replace them.

## Finding C — government/university innovation pipelines already exist
The University of Zimbabwe describes graduate innovator/startup programmes, innovation fellows, intellectual-property assistance and research-to-commercialization support. Its hub reports incubating promising IP and startup formation. NUST's Innovation and Business Development unit supports protection, prototyping, investor links and spinouts. Midlands State University maintains an innovation hub and policy-governed ownership/commercialization. Harare Institute of Technology reported student-origin registered companies from postgraduate coursework in June 2026. Bindura University reports national innovation-hub expansion within Education 5.0.

**Product consequence:** Campus systems are **partners, customers, suppliers of verified information and potential distribution channels**, not empty spaces waiting for a platform to invent entrepreneurship. Each university will have its own IP policies, consent requirements, technology-transfer authority and branding. Never assume a student personally owns an invention simply because they uploaded it.

## Finding D — the National Innovation Acceleration Centre exists
The **National Innovation Acceleration Centre (NIAC)** website describes a national innovation coordination and commercialization initiative established by POTRAZ and the ITU. The site references 2026 innovation-programming, mentoring, grants and national coordination.

**Product consequence:** A new private company should **not** claim to be the nation's first/only innovation platform. Build a focused, superior public experience for students and innovators with consent-based connections to national and university programmes. Consider NIAC as potential ecosystem collaborator rather than represent its public role as a greenfield opportunity.

## Finding E — formidable digital discovery competitors already serve Africa
**VC4A** says its investor search includes project/venture discovery, filters, document exchange, watchlists, alerts and due-diligence support, and publicly states tens of thousands of venture profiles (figures differ across current VC4A pages). **F6S** distributes accelerator/programme opportunities. **Product Hunt** specializes in launches and maker attention. **StartupBlink** tracks public startup ecosystems and maps/rankings. **FINSEC** serves regulated private-market discovery. **Enactus Zimbabwe/BOOST** already organize campus entrepreneurship.

**Product consequence:** Every distinct feature needs a *reason for the user to switch or participate simultaneously*. A defensible proposition might be:
- zero-to-prototype onboarding for students rather than insisting on incorporated startups;
- richer demos and academic/institutional provenance;
- built-in introductions and specific support requests;
- institutional showcase pages and event-to-profile conversion;
- real proof of project progress with claim/source labels;
- low-data Zimbabwean mobile usability;
- practical pathways beyond VC (manufacturing, pilot customers, licensing, mentors, competitions).

## Finding F — African technology investment is real but highly competitive
AVCA's 2025 Venture Capital in Africa research counted 506 venture deals and US$3.9bn in deal value by its coverage; Partech's 2025 Africa tech report uses a different methodology and reports US$4.1bn combined equity/debt across 570 transactions. **These figures are not directly comparable** and neither demonstrates sufficient investor demand in Zimbabwe for every campus project. Do not use continent-wide capital totals to project platform revenue.

**Product consequence:** Revenue should be validated through specific institutional and corporate use cases, not assumed commissions from unrealized fundraising.

## Finding G — design for low bandwidth, not merely high-end dashboards
DataReportal's Digital 2026 Zimbabwe report estimated **6.54m individual internet users (38.4% of population)** at end 2025. POTRAZ subscription-based reports show much higher 'internet penetration' ratios; these are **subscriptions**, not unique human internet users. MISA Zimbabwe discussed the affordability and inclusion gap in August 2026. This distinction matters.

**Product consequence:** mobile-first responsive web/PWA, small images by default, user-controlled video playback, deferred uploads, compression, offline drafts, lightweight share cards, straightforward signup, no mandatory high-resolution video and reasonable language support.

## Where the market appears open (hypotheses to test)
1. A campus-first searchable showcase with persistent project identities accessible globally.
2. A structured, verified university-to-industry introduction workflow that turns a public project into a real meeting, pilot or sponsor.
3. A clear *evidence of progress* layer without false numeric valuations.
4. Student-friendly project creation from PDFs, demos, posters and GitHub projects.
5. Flexible requests: need a cofounder, mentor, university laboratory, materials, manufacturer, research collaboration, pilot customer, grant or investor.
6. Faculty/department challenge boards and showcase events with shareable project pages.
7. Alumni and diaspora discovery (later), if demand is validated.

**Critical warning:** Other services may already contain these elements. The combination is only differentiating if we achieve real campus distribution and useful outcomes; it is not patentable novelty by itself.

## Competitor-positioning matrix
| Platform/organization | Publicly documented strength | Relationship to our idea | Our possible wedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| ZEEX (ZSE) | SME private/public financing, invoice/debt pathways | Regulated finance, potential exit pathway | Student pre-company visibility and qualification |
| FINSEC GEM Portal | Screened enterprise/investor discovery and funding | Direct overlap on investor search & valuation | University-first pipeline & granular creator evidence |
| VC4A | Broad Africa venture/investor network and document due diligence | Direct overlap on profiles, investor matching | Student/research-first, deeper campus workflows |
| F6S | Startup programme/application distribution | Direct overlap on opportunities | Discovery-led showcase + campus context |
| Product Hunt | Public product-launch attention | Inspiration for demo/launch interaction | Continuous university innovation portfolio, not only launch day |
| StartupBlink | Ecosystem mapping and rankings | Overlap on charts/map | Project-level evidence, verified participation and contacts |
| NIAC | National innovation coordination/commercialization | Institutional overlap | Usable creator-facing software + direct project audience |
| UZ, NUST, MSU, HIT hubs | Programme intake, IP, incubation, spinouts | Potential users, partners, at times alternatives | Multi-university discovery and institution-controlled showcase |
| Enactus Zimbabwe / BOOST | Student competitions and entrepreneurship activities | Audience/partner overlap | Durable post-event project identity & partner matching |

## Primary validation gaps
- How do 30 actual students currently showcase work? Why isn't LinkedIn, TikTok, Google Drive or GitHub enough?
- Would university innovation offices officially verify profiles or buy cohort software?
- What do 15 investors and 10 manufacturers demand before requesting an introduction?
- Are student innovators comfortable with public profiles, and how do institutional IP rules affect release?
- Will professionals return monthly without high-volume, *verified* projects?
- Can the platform deliver measurable introductions rather than vanity views?
- What are the operational/legal conditions for sourcing opportunities or forwarding qualified firms to ZEEX, FINSEC or incubators?

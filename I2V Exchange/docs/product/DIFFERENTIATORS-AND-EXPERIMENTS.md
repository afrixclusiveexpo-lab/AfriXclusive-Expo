# Signature Experiences — Product Differentiators to Test

Every idea below is a **testable proposal**, not a patented invention or unique feature claim. Competitors offer pieces of these experiences; our value is continuity across them and local execution quality.

## 1. Project Passport: one identity across every opportunity
The same `project_id` powers public showcase, relevant evidence, milestone journey, competition entry, pitch presentation, sponsor challenge and commercialization outcomes. No duplicate onboarding. Viewers can see an authorized history, not a fabricated 'net worth chart'.

**Moment of delight:** a student enters their second competition in under a minute because the platform reuses their approved project facts; the student's new prototype milestone automatically appears on the project timeline, not in a dead event portal.

**Experiment:** how many entrants complete a second submission compared with re-entering details elsewhere?

## 2. Live Pitch-to-Next-Step
Every pitch ends with *useful* structured requests rather than a generic like count: request demo, share introduction, office hours, ask about a pilot, invite to controlled diligence. The founder sees who asked, why and what will be exposed.

**Moment:** panel Q&A finishes; a real manufacturer requests a consented 20-minute technical follow-up, and the founder accepts without revealing personal contact details in public chat.

**Metric:** proportion of pitches generating mutually accepted relevant follow-up; quality feedback by founders.

## 3. The Challenge-to-Pilot Ladder
Sponsors can post a challenge requiring a measurable solution. Projects progress through concept, feasibility evidence, finalist demo, optional lab test and pilot proposal. IP/terms remain visible; sponsor and creator record what was actually agreed.

**Metric:** % challenges producing genuine evaluations, paid pilots or technical tests rather than only publicity.

## 4. Proof-of-Progress (not streaks)
Milestones have one concise update, stage, optional evidence and a claim label. A creator can ask an authorized mentor/lab to verify a specific fact without handing over the entire project.

**Metric:** sustained progress updates that correlate with real product-development actions; reports of inaccurate verification.

## 5. Campus Innovation Week
A partner organization can create a time-limited showcase of consented university projects, host a competition, issue credible finalist results and archive a reusable season page; student identities continue afterward.

**Metric:** partner organizer repeats programme; creator projects keep receiving useful contact requests afterward.

## 6. Adaptive First-Impression Cards
Cards adapt to user intent without hiding key facts: student sees demo + collaborator needs; investor sees stage + evidence scope; engineer sees technical demonstration. All variants come from the same approved **public source**, never different permission paths.

**Metric:** qualified contact rate vs misleading click-through and bounced visitors.

## 7. Opportunity Confidence Cards
Instead of an unexplained '97% match', show why a project may fit a grant/competition: sector, project stage, geography, deadline, team requirements; emphasize what's unverified.

**Metric:** eligible submitted applications and false-positive recommendation complaints.

## 8. Collaboration Mission Board
Founders can publish narrowly defined needs, e.g. 'embedded systems collaborator for 6-week sensor prototype'. Applicants see scope, status, terms (academic/volunteer/paid/cofounder), onboarding expectations and legal rights disclosures.

**Metric:** meaningful team matches and completed contributions.

## 9. Demonstration Receipts
After a legitimate live demo, a moderator or evaluator may issue a limited record such as 'demonstration occurred on date', not 'technology proven commercially viable'. The record is linkable, issuer-specific, correctable and revocable.

**Metric:** trust perception and credential challenge/correction rate.

## 10. University Innovation Syndication
With permission, institutions can showcase selected public projects on their own website through controlled embeds/API, keeping profile identity stable and attribution correct. Institution administrators cannot thereby gain access to confidential student materials.

**Metric:** search discovery via partner site, student opt-in and update consistency.

## 11. 'Missed the prize, found the partner'
After competitions, entrants can opt in to relevant feedback, intros and non-winning project discovery. Judges may mark technical interest while awards stay separate.

**Metric:** non-winning teams receiving actionable follow-up; fairness and privacy complaints.

## 12. Community Labs and Prototype Days
Run in-person demos with QR codes linking to approved project pages, scheduled pitch sessions, physical attendance optional and online viewers on bandwidth-friendly streams. The platform maintains a continuous record afterward.

**Metric:** projects receiving post-event contacts and external testing opportunities.

## Implementation ordering
P0 test 1 + 4 + curated cards. P1 test 2 + 3 + 5. P2 test 6–12 as distribution, institutions and data justify it.

## Product anti-gimmick rule
If a feature increases app engagement but does not improve project discovery, development, credible evidence, collaboration or opportunity access, it must justify its place or be removed. Do not build token rewards, fake trading charts or leaderboards designed solely for addiction.
